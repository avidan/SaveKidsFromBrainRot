🛡️ SaveKidsFromBrainRot — Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 2026

SaveKidsFromBrainRot is an open-source, self-hosted parental-control system for YouTube. Each family runs its own server; there is no central service and no company collecting your data. This policy describes what the browser extension does with data on the devices where a parent installs it.

What the extension collects

On YouTube pages only, the extension reads video and channel information (titles, channel names, video IDs, thumbnails) and records viewing events: videos watched, videos blocked, "ask my grown-up" requests, and time spent watching. It does not read pages on any other site, does not capture keystrokes, passwords, or form input, and does not access the camera or microphone.

Where the data goes

All data is sent to one place: the server the parent paired the device with — a server the family deploys and controls (typically on their own Cloudflare account). Nothing is sent anywhere else by the extension. Before a device is paired, the extension transmits nothing at all.

AI evaluation

To judge content against the family's rules, the family's server sends video/channel metadata (titles, descriptions, thumbnails, and sometimes transcript excerpts — all public YouTube data) to Anthropic's Claude API using the family's own API key, subject to Anthropic's privacy policy. No information about the child (name, account, identity) is included in these requests.

What is never done with your data

  • Never sold or shared with third parties.
  • Never used for advertising, analytics, or profiling.
  • Never used for any purpose other than enforcing the family's own rules and showing parents their family's activity.

Retention and deletion

Activity data lives in the family's own database and is retained until the family deletes it. Deleting the server (or revoking a device from the dashboard) ends all collection. Local caches on the device are removed by uninstalling the extension.

Children's data

The system is operated by parents/guardians for their own children on devices they own. The data collected (viewing activity) is visible only to the family operating the server.
